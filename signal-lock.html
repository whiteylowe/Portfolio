The scenario
Plan a drone's route on a tablet, then look up and refine its destination through wearable AR—without losing the target or the task.
The design question
How can an instruction move from a screen-based overview into the physical world, while keeping its meaning and feedback intact?

02 / From screen to world

The tablet is for the big picture. Wearable AR could help at the moment a task becomes local: checking a shoreline, refining a point beyond a tree, or redirecting the drone from where the operator stands.

Shared target / different interfaces

Imagine selecting a coastal waypoint on the tablet. As the drone approaches, you look up through AR glasses and recognize the same target in the landscape.

Rather than navigate back through a map, you refine the instruction from your own perspective: move beyond that tree, scan this area, hold position.

The interaction principle is continuity. One task should remain recognizable as the controls and available screen space change.

Interface behavior studies

These animated studies investigate four parts of the experience. They illustrate interaction principles, not sensor-verified gaze tracking or autonomous drone perception.

Attention + Targeting
Proposed gaze-guided selection
A reticle holds a candidate location in view. Looking can suggest a target; an explicit action would confirm a drone command.
Situated Guidance
Only what matters nearby
A compact cue helps the operator orient to a location without reproducing the entire tablet interface.
Scan + Feedback
Visible task progress
Sampling animation and markers communicate what the drone is inspecting and how the task is progressing.
Information Priority
Overview vs. first-person
Routes and status can spread out on the tablet, while the wearable view prioritizes direction, selection, and alerts.
The shared interaction model

The intended relationship is not two unrelated interfaces. It's one drone task represented differently: spatial overview on the tablet and immediate direction and feedback in AR.

Commands, feedback + task states

A useful drone interface should make the next action unambiguous: select a destination, choose an operation, receive feedback, then adjust or cancel. The following studies explore visual language for those moments.

PROPOSED / NOT YET HARDWARE TESTED

The larger XR concept is a handoff between tablet planning and situated command in wearable AR.

  • Live coordination of target and task state between both displays.
  • World-anchored AR cues and actual eye-tracking input.
  • Drone integration and real-world navigation trials.
Wearable AR / proposed experience

The design opportunity is simple: issue an instruction from the big picture, then finish it from where you're standing. Signal Lock is an early exploration of how those two scales of interaction might connect.
